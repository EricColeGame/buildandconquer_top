## Understanding City Progression and How Build and Conquer Levels Work

Progression in the game revolves around your City Level. Unlike traditional strategy games where levels are determined by an arbitrary experience bar or pure combat victories, advancing your town hall status relies on community-reported civilian metrics—most notably, total sustained population.

According to community wiki data and player reports, city level increases trigger automatically or via the city management interface as soon as set conditions are satisfied. These prerequisites are predominantly population-oriented. When your population crosses designated thresholds and basic stability is maintained, your nation upgrades, unlocking higher-tier technology, denser housing alternatives, heavier industrial facilities, and lucrative commercial establishments.

Advancing your **Build and Conquer levels** provides three primary operational advantages:

*   **Spatial Density:** Early housing options take up massive footprints while housing very few citizens. Higher levels introduce multi-story apartments and high-rises that deliver massive headcounts on compact footprints.
*   **Industrial & Resource Capabilities:** Advanced civic levels introduce buildings that require refined materials like steel and uranium, paving the way for advanced manufacturing.
*   **Economic Scale:** High-level commercial structures, such as corporate office towers and banks, generate the immense recurring revenue needed to fund national defenses and utility upkeeps.

## Early Game Milestones: Maximizing Growth in Levels 1 and 2

Navigating the starting **Build and Conquer levels** requires careful cash flow management. When you drop onto an empty plot, your treasury is limited, and building unchecked infrastructure can easily drag your economy into the red.

In Level 1, your main objective is establishing a baseline population while keeping utilities positive. Every residential unit demands continuous access to electricity and water. If either utility fails, residential buildings stop generating income and can cause population counts to fluctuate wildly.

### Level 1 Housing Strategy

During the earliest stage, players rely on basic residential houses. While single-family dwellings are cheap upfront, they consume considerable land.

*Community wiki data* records the following early residential properties:

*   **Poor House:** Costs $50 with a 16-second build time. Delivers +3 Population and +$4 per pay cycle while using 5 Energy and requiring an active water supply.
*   **Standard House / Suburban Houses (Types 1–9):** Generally cost between $130 and $202. They provide +5 Population and +$10 per pay cycle, each needing 5 Energy and water coverage.
*   **Two-Story Suburban Variants:** Cost between $170 and $275, yielding +8 Population and up to +$10 per cycle for the same 5 Energy demand.

### Early Utility Setup

For early power, Solar Panels ($100, providing 20 Energy with no upkeep) and Wind Turbines ($500, providing 200 Energy with -$2 pay cycle upkeep) are your best friends. Never neglect substations ($700), which are necessary to relay electricity further away from your generation sources across the building grid. 

For water, Water Towers cost a modest $12 and provide water within a 500x500 stud radius to serve up to 200 citizens, adding +12 happiness. Alternatively, players aiming for rapid early scaling invest in a Desalination Plant ($5,000, requiring 200 Energy and -$25/cycle upkeep) to secure city-wide water coverage early.

### Unlocking Level 2

As your population expands past initial requirements, Level 2 unlocks substantially more efficient housing options and municipal services. Instead of scattering dozens of detached single-family homes, you gain access to apartment complexes.

## Mid to Late Game Transition: Scaling Infrastructure in Levels 3 and 4

Pushing toward higher **Build and Conquer levels** introduces heavy industry requirements and major resource supply chains. At Level 1 and Level 2, money and energy are your primary limiting factors. Starting at Level 3, buildings begin demanding refined industrial materials—principally Steel.

### The Role of Steel in Level 3

At Level 3, your city can construct mid-tier retail, specialized offices, and dense urban housing. However, constructing a City Apartment ($2,200, +20 Pop, +$80/cycle) requires 22 Steel alongside cash. Similarly, a Large Supermarket ($2,400) requires 24 Steel and 8 civilian workers, generating +$260 per cycle while providing +13 happiness to 100 nearby citizens.

Municipal security also becomes essential. While Level 2 introduced the modest Sheriff's Office ($500, 4 workers, -$100 upkeep), Level 3 introduces the Municipal Precinct ($20,000, 50 Energy, 20 workers, 200 Steel, -$1,000 upkeep). Neglecting safety infrastructure as your city scales can cripple population satisfaction and impede progress toward higher tiers.

### Level 4 Modernization and the Nuclear Grid

Reaching Level 4 represents a massive qualitative leap. You transition from suburban sprawl into a genuine metropolis featuring high-rise towers, multi-story office complexes, and massive power generation.

However, power consumption skyrockets in Level 4. Traditional wind farms and Level 2 Coal Power Plants ($5,000, providing 6,000 Energy but consuming coal reserves and -$18/cycle upkeep) can no longer sustain the demands of high-rise buildings. Level 4 unlocks the Nuclear Power Plant:

*   **Construction Cost:** $50,000 + 500 Steel
*   **Build Time:** 80 seconds
*   **Output:** 60,000 Energy
*   **Running Costs:** -1 Uranium per minute, -$120 per pay cycle

## Common Bottlenecks When Climbing Build and Conquer Levels (and How to Fix Them)

Many mayors hit an unexpected wall while trying to unlock higher **Build and Conquer levels**. Often, a player reaches several hundred residents or builds dozens of residential homes, yet the game refuses to promote the city to Level 2 or Level 3.

Here are the most frequent bottlenecks documented in community discussions and how to troubleshoot them:

### 1. Ghost Population Deficits
Building a house reserves population capacity, but citizens only move in and remain counted if their basic needs are met. If a sector of your city loses water pressure or falls outside power grid coverage, those houses become inactive. Always verify that your water towers cover all residential neighborhoods and that substations are actively linked to working power plants.

### 2. The Worker Shortage Trap
Commercial buildings (shops, gas stations, office towers) require unassigned civilian workers. If you build too many commercial structures without sufficient residential density, your workforce becomes stretched thin. When commercial buildings lack sufficient staff, their tax yields plummet, starving you of the funds needed for city upgrades.

### 3. Steel Production Stalls
As noted earlier, transitioning to Level 3 and Level 4 relies heavily on steel. If your industrial supply chain is nonexistent, you will find yourself unable to construct modern apartments or offices. Balance your economy so that industrial extraction and refining start well before you intend to lay down metropolitan skyscrapers.

### 4. Severe Happiness Penalties
Certain high-yield buildings, like Communal Housing, provide large population boosts at a steep happiness penalty (-14 happiness). If national happiness drops too low, overall efficiency suffers. Counteract negative happiness modifiers by placing parks, retail shops, supermarkets, and ensuring adequate police coverage via Sheriff's Offices or Municipal Precincts.

Balancing revenue and population is the core rhythm for clearing **Build and Conquer levels** smoothly. Keep your utilities ahead of demand, reserve open space for multi-tile industrial hubs, and upgrade systematically rather than over-expanding prematurely.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Build and Conquer Levels

### Why is my city stuck between Build and Conquer levels even with high population?
In player experience reports, reaching a specific resident headcount is the primary condition, but your population must be active and satisfied. If homes lack water, energy, or necessary road connections, that headcount may not register toward the level upgrade threshold. Ensure all utility networks are green, verify that your police and safety services meet local demands, and check your city interface to confirm all requirements are met.

### Can I skip building single-family houses and wait for high-density apartments?
No. High-density residential options like Low Income Apartments or City Apartments remain locked behind Level 2, Level 3, and Level 4. You must build low-tier housing (such as basic houses or two-story suburban homes) during Level 1 to build the initial population necessary to unlock advanced housing options. Once higher tiers are unlocked, you can bulldoze early homes to make room for skyscrapers.

### What is the maximum city level in Build and Conquer?
Because *Build and Conquer* is actively developed in Beta (V0.1) by The Slop Company, maximum level caps and progression requirements may change across updates. Community gameplay videos currently document progression through Level 4 and Level 5 cities. Players looking for the newest balance adjustments should verify active development notes on the developer's official channels.

### Do military units and weapons unlock directly through Build and Conquer levels?
Indirectly, yes. While military units, firearms, and missiles require direct technology research and specialized factories (like barracks and munitions plants), advanced military infrastructure and high-tier defenses depend on the economic backing, energy grids, and refined resources (like steel and uranium) unlocked as your city reaches higher levels. Raising your city level is essential to sustain a formidable military force.
