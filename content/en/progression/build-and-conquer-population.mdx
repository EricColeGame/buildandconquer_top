## Why Your Build and Conquer Population Drives Overall Progression

In Build and Conquer, territory leveling is fundamentally population-oriented. You cannot unlock advanced weapon factories, missile silos, or heavy vehicle bays simply by hoarding cash. Progression requires meeting explicit civilian thresholds that unlock higher City Levels.

Scaling your **Build and Conquer population** is directly linked to four major gameplay mechanics:

- **City Level Advancement:** Higher city tiers unlock advanced construction options, ranging from multi-story office towers to nuclear power plants.
- **Military Capability:** According to player reports and wiki benchmarks, reaching 250 residents is the critical milestone required to unlock Level 2 and establish your first dedicated military base.
- **Workforce Availability:** Heavy industrial assets, commercial storefronts, police stations, and refineries require civilian workers to operate. Without spare citizens, these buildings shut down.
- **Tax and Revenue Streams:** Every residential structure supplies recurring income each pay cycle, creating a self-funding loop that finances expansion.

When you claim an empty plot, land space and starting capital are your tightest bottlenecks. In the early game, your primary objective is placing high-efficiency residential zones along cleanly planned road corridors. Early single-family dwellings require minimal utilities while steadily boosting your workforce.

Choosing the right housing to raise your **Build and Conquer population** early depends on balancing construction cost against residential capacity. While basic suburban homes cost between $130 and $136, two-story variants offer superior population-per-footprint ratios.

### The Communal Housing Trap

Player experience highlights a common mistake: mass-producing **Communal Housing 1**. Although it provides an impressive +44 population for under $4,000, it inflicts a massive **-14 happiness penalty**. 

If cumulative city happiness drops too low, citizens abandon your nation, causing spontaneous depopulation. Counterbalance dense, lower-tier housing with commercial retail stores, water infrastructure, and positive-happiness complexes like Suburban or Mid-Rise apartments.

## Late-Game Population Strategies and Territory Buffs

Once your base settlement is stable, scaling your population into the thousands requires territory control and defensive measures. 

### Capturing Mainland Control Points

A great shortcut to boost your **Build and Conquer population** cap is deploying forces through transit tunnels onto the shared mainland. Capturing and holding strategic mainland sectors grants immediate empire-wide bonuses:

- **+30% Population Capacity Boost:** Instantly raises your national civilian ceiling, allowing for denser apartment construction.
- **+20% Cash Income Multiplier:** Enhances total tax yields, funding expensive high-rise residential projects and nuclear power grids.

Keep in mind that deploying units to the mainland activates open player-versus-player (PvP) combat, leaving your territory open to enemy counter-offensives.

### Protecting Citizens from Bombardment

High-population cities are prime targets for rival airstrikes and missile artillery. If an adversary launches rockets into your residential core, civilian buildings collapse, wiping out your workforce in seconds.

- **Construct Bomb Shelters:** Unlocked at Level 1 for $3,000, each shelter protects up to 500 citizens during air raids and missile strikes.
- **Air Raid Sirens:** Install sirens ($250) along housing districts to give your population early warning to seek shelter.
- **Layered Air Defense:** Position point-defense installations (such as Sea RAM units and anti-air interceptors) around apartment blocks to protect your **Build and Conquer population** from devastating missile strikes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my Build and Conquer population declining?
A declining population is almost always caused by negative city happiness or collapsed utility networks. If water towers run out of coverage, power plants lack fuel, or you overbuild happiness-draining structures like Communal Housing (-14 happiness), citizens will move out. Restore power, ensure every house has water, and build retail shops to keep your **Build and Conquer population** satisfied.

### How much population do I need to reach Level 2 in Build and Conquer?
According to community wiki records and player reports, you need approximately 250 citizens to meet the condition for City Level 2. Reaching this milestone unlocks crucial infrastructure, including your first military base, coal power plants, and medium-density apartments.

### What is the most efficient residential building for early population growth?
The Two Story House ($170, 16s build time) is widely considered the best early-game option. It provides +8 population for only 5 energy and a water connection, offering far better density and cost efficiency than standard single-family suburban models.

### Does capturing mainland sectors help with population?
Yes. Holding strategic mainland control points provides a reported +30% boost to population capacity alongside a +20% income bonus. However, sending units through the tunnels flags your territory for real-time multiplayer combat, so ensure your air defenses and civil bomb shelters are active first.
