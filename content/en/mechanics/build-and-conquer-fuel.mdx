## Understanding How Fuel Works in Build and Conquer

In *Build and Conquer*, fuel is not an ambient resource that automatically trickles into your treasury. Instead, it functions as a refined production material sitting directly in the middle of a multi-tiered manufacturing tree. 

Before a single drop of fuel reaches your motor pools, missile silos, or heavy military factories, you must construct an interconnected web of basic utilities, residential workforce housing, and resource extraction facilities. Community wiki data indicates that failing to support even one link in this logistics chain—such as letting power grids brown out or running low on clean water—causes processing buildings to idle immediately.

```
[Crude Oil Deposit] ──► [Oil Pump] ──► [Refinery] ──► [Fuel Storage / Military Base]
                             ▲              ▲
                      Power & Roads   Power & Workers
```

Fuel serves two overarching operational functions: sustaining economic transport and powering the assembly lines that produce motorized ground vehicles, missile systems, and strike drones. Without steady fuel income, your industrial plants cannot fulfill production queues, leaving your base unable to field units or reinforce frontline skirmishes through the transit tunnels.

The table below outlines the core economic hierarchy that governs resource refinement in the current game version:

| Resource Category | Primary Role in Base Economy | Key Production Sources | Consequences of a Severe Deficit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basic Utilities** | Powers machines and supports the workforce | Solar Panels, Wind Turbines, Coal Plants, Water Tanks | Population collapses; extraction plants shut down |
| **Raw Resources** | Raw materials required for industrial processing | Oil Pumps, Coal and Iron Mines | Processing facilities idle; city growth slows |

| **Military Hardware** | Direct tactical units and defensive armaments | Military Factories, SRBM/MRBM Launchers | Inability to defend territory or contest mainland sectors |

## Step-by-Step Infrastructure: Producing Fuel from Scratch

Constructing a self-sufficient fuel economy requires discipline during the early stages of base layout. Rushing straight into industrial refineries without adequate civic backing is the single most common cause of early economic failure reported by players.

### Step 1: Establish Reliable Utilities and Housing
Every industrial building requires operational power and a steady workforce. If your citizens lack water or electricity, residential structures will depopulate, stripping your refineries of the laborers needed to convert crude oil into fuel.

*   **Power Generation:** Lay down Solar Panels or Wind Turbines early. As demand rises, transition toward Coal Power Plants to support heavy industrial loads.
*   **Water Management:** Construct Water Tanks or Desalination Factories near residential zones to maintain maximum population happiness and steady tax revenue.
*   **Zoning Residential Blocks:** Build Mid-Rise Apartments or Suburban Houses along well-connected paved roads to supply your municipal job market.

### Step 2: Survey and Extract Crude Oil
Crude oil serves as the mandatory precursor for all fuel manufacturing. Players must locate designated resource nodes across their building plot and construct dedicated extraction equipment directly on top of them.

*   Place **Oil Pumps** directly on verified raw deposits.
*   Ensure every Oil Pump connects cleanly to your roadway grid so extracted barrels can transfer downstream.
*   Verify that your power grid has sufficient surplus capacity to power the pumps around the clock.

### Step 3: Refine Raw Deposits into Processed Fuel
Once crude oil is pumping, you must erect processing infrastructure to convert those raw barrels into usable fuel canisters. According to player experience, placing refineries along high-capacity road corridors near your extraction zones dramatically minimizes transit delay.

The table below summarizes the key industrial structures tied to crude oil extraction and fuel refining:

| Building Name | Category | Primary Function | Community Notes & Observations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oil Pump** | Resource Extraction | Extracts raw crude oil from surface/ground deposits | Requires active power grid connection and road access |
| **Small Refinery** | Industrial Processing | Converts basic crude oil into refined fuel | Best for early-game maintenance and baseline stockpiling |
| **Medium Refinery** | Advanced Industrial | High-throughput fuel refining for expanded militaries | Higher power draw; requires a substantial workforce pool |
| **Small Factory** | Light Industrial | Processes basic resources into consumer goods | Generates commercial trade materials alongside refineries |
| **Large Military Factory**| Military Production | Consumes refined fuel and steel to build combat units | Heavy resource drain; stalls instantly during fuel shortages |

## Balancing Fuel Economy Across City Tiers

As your settlement expands, your fuel strategy must adjust to accommodate higher operational consumption. Managing your **Build and Conquer fuel** balance requires anticipating demand spikes long before you issue unit construction commands.

```
City Tier 1 (Foundations)      City Tier 2 (Industrialization)     City Tier 3+ (Warfare)
   - Baseline utilities          - Oil Pumps & Small Refineries      - Medium Refineries
   - Low fuel priority           - Building initial reserves         - Sustained mainland sorties
```

In the early game, fuel consumption remains virtually non-existent because early progression centers on municipal expansion, commercial supermarkets, and population growth. However, transitioning into City Level 2 and Level 3 introduces massive fuel overhead as advanced factories and combat installations come online.

The table below breaks down fuel management priorities across each operational tier:

| Progression Stage | Fuel Priority Level | Core Infrastructure Focus | Early Warning Signs of Imbalance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Settlement Opening** | Low | Roads, Mid-Rise Apartments, Water Tanks, Wind Turbines | Negative cash flow; empty workforce slots in commercial stores |
| **Industrial Expansion** | Moderate | First Oil Pumps, Small Refineries, Warehouses | Extraction rigs idling; power brownouts across utility grids |
| **Military Buildup** | High | Medium Refineries, Sloparms Offices, Military Factories | Manufacturing queues stalling midway through vehicle cycles |
| **Mainland Warfare** | Critical | Redundant fuel lines, fortified storage, reserve pipelines | Offensive armor units stranded at base; empty missile silos |

Community reports emphasize that players should achieve stable surplus production before committing to military contracts. If your reserve buffer is razor-thin, fielding a single convoy of armored vehicles can drain your reserves completely, causing municipal systems to freeze in cascade.

## Troubleshooting Fuel Bottlenecks and Production Halts

Even experienced players occasionally encounter situations where fuel gauges refuse to fill despite placing refineries. In almost every scenario, the problem originates from an overlooked link in the broader logistical chain rather than a bug.

If your fuel numbers stop climbing, evaluate your base layout against the troubleshooting matrix below:

| Observed Symptom | Underlying System Bottleneck | Recommended Strategic Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Refineries completely idle** | Missing or disconnected road connection to Oil Pumps | Re-lay road segments between extraction wells and processing plants |
| **Output dropping periodically** | Power grid fluctuations or coal plant deficits | Build extra Solar Panels or Wind Turbines to create a 20% power buffer |
| **Slow refining cycles** | Insufficient workforce in the industrial district | Zone additional Mid-Rise Apartments; ensure residential water needs are met |
| **Production stops at fixed number**| Storage capacity reached maximum limit | Upgrade storage tanks or expend reserves in manufacturing queues |
| **Sudden negative income crash** | Military upkeep outpacing commercial tax revenue | Pause unit construction; construct Supermarkets to restore positive cash flow |

Maintaining a dedicated buffer is the most reliable defense against catastrophic shortages. Experienced builders recommend leaving at least 15% to 25% surplus capacity in energy and municipal water supplies before launching extensive refinery expansion projects.

## Build and Conquer Fuel FAQ

### How do I get Build and Conquer fuel in the early game?
To obtain **Build and Conquer fuel**, you must first reach a stable municipal foundation with power, water, and residential housing. Next, place an Oil Pump on a designated crude oil deposit, connect it via paved roads to your primary district, and construct a Small Refinery to begin converting raw oil into usable fuel.

### Why did my fuel refinery stop producing resources?
Refineries stop operating when they lose road connections to crude oil sources, run out of available workers due to declining residential happiness, or experience electrical brownouts. Additionally, check your storage limit: if your stockpiles are full, refining will pause until materials are consumed.

### Can enemy players raid my fuel reserves?
During active PvP phases on the shared mainland, hostile nations can target forward logistics nodes and military assets. While primary home-base plots enjoy certain territorial protections, vulnerable supply lines and forward bases can be contested and disrupted during aggressive raids.

### What is the most efficient way to scale fuel for missile production?
The most reliable method is constructing Medium Refineries paired with multiple dedicated Oil Pumps, backed by Coal Power Plants to prevent energy shortfalls. Always establish a substantial stockpile of refined materials before queuing multiple heavy missile or advanced vehicle builds.
