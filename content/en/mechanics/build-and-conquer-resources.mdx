## The Core Economy: How Build and Conquer Resources Power Expansion

The game's economy functions through cyclical production loops. Unlike basic sandbox games where money simply accumulates endlessly, your city’s cash flow depends directly on balancing residential growth, commercial profits, and utility overheads. Every building you place imposes pay cycle upkeep fees, energy demands, and water dependencies.

Community reports show that new mayors frequently bankrupt themselves within their first thirty minutes. This collapse usually happens when players construct heavy defensive infrastructure or advanced office complexes before their baseline civilian economy can support them. 

```
[Water + Energy] ➔ [Housing / Population] ➔ [Labor Force] ➔ [Commercial & Industrial Output] ➔ [Taxes & Steel] ➔ [Military Defense]
```

To progress cleanly through City Levels, you must understand your balance sheet. Money is generated each pay cycle by commercial venues and populated residences, while maintenance and supply chains draw funds away. If your net income dips into negative numbers, your construction halts, factories stall, and your municipal grid fails.

## Essential Utilities: Managing Energy and Water Infrastructure

Before mining minerals or manufacturing advanced munitions, you must secure municipal lifelines: electricity and clean water. Without electricity, commercial zones cannot operate. Without water, residences lose occupants, erasing both your tax base and workforce.

### Power Generation Systems

Energy distribution is local unless extended. Power plants feature localized ranges, meaning you must place substations to route current deeper into your city plots. In early setups, clean green energy eliminates fuel logistics, but high-density skyscrapers and defense systems require heavy grid power.

*Community wiki data highlights the following performance profiles for early and late-game energy generators:*

| Energy Building | Build Cost | Build Time | Energy Output | Upkeep / Fuel Requirements | City Level Unlock |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solar Panel** | $100 | 12s | 20 Energy | None (Zero fuel & upkeep) | Level 1 |
| **Wind Turbine** | $500 | 24s | 200 Energy | -$2 / Pay cycle | Level 1 |
| **Substation** | $700 | 16s | Power Relay | -$2 / Pay cycle (Needs plant range) | Level 1 |
| **Coal Power Plant** | $5,000 | 32s | 6,000 Energy | -$18 / Pay cycle + Burns Coal | Level 2 |
| **Nuclear Power Plant** | $50,000 | 80s | 60,000 Energy | -$120 / Pay cycle, 500 Steel, -1 Uranium/min | Level 4 |

Early expansion benefits greatly from Wind Turbines. While Solar Panels are maintenance-free, their low output consumes excessive plot space. Once you transition to Coal Power Plants at Level 2, establish steady raw mineral lines to avoid catastrophic blackouts.

### Water Distribution Networks

Water supply behaves as an absolute gatekeeper for population capacity. If a house lacks running water, citizens leave, triggering labor shortages across nearby retail stores and refineries.

| Water Structure | Build Cost | Coverage / Capacity | Upkeep Costs | Key Requirements |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Water Tower** | $12 | 200 Citizens (500x500 area) | -$2 / Pay cycle | Local placement |
| **Desalination Plant** | $5,000 | Unlimited City-wide coverage | -$25 / Pay cycle | 200 Energy (+1,000 Energy per citizen scaling) |

Player experience suggests placing localized Water Towers during early developmental milestones. The Desalination Plant provides unlimited city-wide piping, but its exponential energy scaling can overload your early electrical supply if your population expands faster than your power plants.

## Industrial Supply Chains: Raw Materials, Fuel, and Steel Production

Extracting and converting raw minerals constitutes the backbone of mid-game national strength. Advanced construction and missile programs cannot rely on base cash alone; they demand physical commodities produced inside industrial sectors.

Managing downstream **Build and Conquer resources** like steel and refined fuel allows your city to transition from a defenseless municipal grid into a fortified power.

```
Mines (Coal & Iron) ───────► Industrial Factories ────► Structural Steel & Consumer Goods
Oil Pumps (Crude Oil) ─────► Refineries ──────────────► Refined Fuel & Munitions Supply
```

### Key Raw and Refined Commodities

*   **Coal:** Mined from natural deposits; serves as combustible fuel for standard thermal plants and industrial processing.
*   **Iron Ore:** Extracted via community mines; directly feeds smelters to produce finished structural metals.
*   **Crude Oil:** Pumped from field reserves; transported to industrial sectors for fuel conversion.
*   **Refined Fuel:** Processed in Small and Medium Refineries; vital for vehicle maneuvers, logistics, and heavy transport.
*   **Steel:** Advanced structural alloy required for mid-to-high-tier office towers, municipal infrastructure, and defense batteries.
*   **Uranium:** High-tier fissile material consumed on a per-minute basis to run nuclear stations.

## Balancing the Workforce: Residential Density vs. Commercial Profit

Every commercial property requires two inputs: utility connectivity and available workers. Workers come strictly from your housing districts. Striking the balance between residential expansion and retail employment stabilizes your pay cycle profits.

Player reports indicate that placing too many high-density offices too soon triggers workforce deficits. When an office lacks workers, it produces no tax income while continuing to deduct its static maintenance fees every cycle.

## Defense and Warfare: Converting Domestic Wealth into Firepower

A prosperous economy will quickly attract attention from neighboring players. Tunnels lead directly out toward the contested mainland, meaning your domestic infrastructure remains vulnerable to missile barrages, drone swarms, and mechanized raids. 

Stockpiling critical **Build and Conquer resources** allows you to erect air defense perimeters and manufacture retaliatory weaponry. 

```
Domestic Economy ($) ──► Military Research ──► Munitions & Missile Launchers ──► Mainland Superiority
```

### Military Infrastructure and Production Facilities

*   **Barracks:** Recruits ground personnel for regional skirmishes and tunnel operations.
*   **Military Factories & Vehicle Storage:** Converts domestic refined steel and fuel into combat transport vehicles, armored utility units, and logistical trucks.
*   **Sloparms Offices (Small / Medium / Large):** Administrative research centers that unlock advanced warfare doctrines, guidance hardware, and defense systems.
*   **Air Defense Batteries (Iron Dome, Patriot Battery, Patriot PAC-3):** Automated surface-to-air systems designed to intercept incoming tactical rockets, hypersonic ordnance, and Shahed strike drones.
*   **Missile Warfare Sites (SRBM & MRBM Launchers):** High-yield strategic weapons capable of targeting rival industrial clusters, desalination facilities, and defensive batteries across the map.

Air defense systems carry substantial operating overhead. If a surprise assault severs your power grid, your Iron Dome batteries shut down immediately, leaving the rest of your city defenseless. Group backup wind generators and substations near critical military plots to keep defensive radar arrays online during power cuts.

## 4 Essential Rules for Resource Management

1.  **Never Outpace Your Power Surplus:** Maintain a 15–20% energy buffer. Adding new factories or desalination systems without checking grid headspace causes cascading brownouts across your city.
2.  **Separate Industrial Pollution from Housing:** Placing mines and heavy factories immediately beside residential neighborhoods triggers happiness penalties that reduce overall tax yields.
3.  **Buffer Your Emergency Cash Reserves:** Do not spend your treasury down to zero on offensive weapons. Keep at least five pay cycles worth of cash reserves to absorb unexpected infrastructure repairs after an air raid.
4.  **Route Utilities Before Upgrading:** Always place roads, substations, and water networks before dropping expensive office blueprints.

By maintaining disciplined supply lines, balancing civic housing against commercial demand, and protecting heavy industry with layered air defense networks, your city will thrive through every update and conflict.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I do if my money drains rapidly into negative numbers?
A rapidly draining treasury happens when maintenance costs outpace commercial revenue, usually because buildings lack workers or electricity. Pause military construction immediately. Check your commercial buildings: if an office displays an "insufficient workers" or "unpowered" alert, it charges upkeep without yielding profits. Demolish excess high-upkeep buildings or rapidly construct standard housing within water coverage to supply the necessary labor pool.

### How do I unlock Level 2 and Level 3 buildings in Build and Conquer?
Advancing city levels requires satisfying population benchmarks, positive pay cycle balance, and municipal services like law enforcement. Community reports suggest reaching Level 2 demands stable civilian population figures and safety structures like the Sheriff's Office ($500), alongside consistent utility delivery across all occupied zones.

### Where do I obtain Steel to build advanced office towers and military hardware?
Steel is a refined intermediate material manufactured by industrial factories. You must extract iron and coal via mines, then feed those materials into operational factories. Once processed, steel automatically banks into your construction inventory for use in advanced commercial towers, municipal precincts, and missile installations.

### What are the most critical Build and Conquer resources for defending against missile attacks?
Early missile defense relies on energy, capital, and researched military tech. Maintaining redundant power generators prevents anti-air batteries from going dark during shelling. Once researched through Sloparms tech trees, placing Iron Dome or Patriot PAC-3 installations creates an intercept envelope, provided your city generates enough steady cash to pay their pay cycle maintenance.
